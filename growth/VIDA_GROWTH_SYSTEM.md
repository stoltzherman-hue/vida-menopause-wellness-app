# Vida owned growth system

## North star

Help South African women who are wondering whether their symptoms may relate to perimenopause discover a trustworthy free tool, complete a first check-in, and return often enough to see a useful personal pattern.

The first 30 days optimise for learning, not vanity. No invented testimonials, inflated community counts, bought followers, automated direct messages or medical promises.

## Funnel and weekly scorecard

| Stage | Event or source | First target | Decision rule |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| Discover | Vercel visits by source and landing page | 500 qualified visits | Double down only on posts that produce engaged visits |
| Consider | Signup page visits | 20% of homepage visits | If lower, sharpen the promise and CTA |
| Activate | Confirmed accounts | 20% of signup starts | If lower, inspect email delivery and confirmation friction |
| First value | `growth.onboarding.completed` | 70% of confirmed accounts | If lower, shorten or clarify onboarding |
| Habit | `growth.checkin.completed` on 3 separate days | 35% of activated accounts | If lower, improve the first-week prompts and feedback |
| Community | First genuine post or reply | 10% of activated accounts | Seed questions, never fake activity |

Report counts and conversion rates together. With small samples, show the denominator and do not over-interpret percentage movement.

## Channel focus

Start with two owned social surfaces and one search surface:

1. Instagram: saveable carousels, short reels, founder notes and product demonstrations.
2. Facebook: an official page plus respectful participation in relevant South African communities where group rules permit it.
3. Vida Knowledge Hub: one strong search-led guide refreshed or published each week, with direct links to WHO, NICE, NHS or ACOG where clinical claims appear.

Do not open six dormant channels. Secure the same handle on YouTube and TikTok only if available; leave them unlaunched until the content loop works.

## Positioning

Primary promise: “Understand your menopause patterns.”

Supporting promise: “A private two-minute daily check-in, a clearer record for healthcare conversations, and a community that is honest about being new.”

Call to action: “Start your free check-in.”

Proof we can use now:

- The product exists and the free tracking flow works.
- Community is free.
- Health data is not sold.
- Educational articles link to recognised public health guidance.

Proof we cannot use yet:

- Customer outcomes or quotations without written permission.
- Large community or active-user claims.
- Claims that tracking, CBT, breathing or HRT will produce a specific result for an individual.
- “Clinician reviewed” until a named qualified reviewer has actually reviewed the content.

## Content pillars

1. “Is this perimenopause?” — symptom recognition without diagnosis.
2. “Track the pattern” — simple prompts linking sleep, mood, triggers and symptoms.
3. “Prepare to be heard” — appointment-preparation checklists and respectful self-advocacy.
4. “What the guidance says” — plain-language summaries with direct sources.
5. “Building Vida in public” — honest founder updates, product demos and invitations to shape the product.

Every health post must include a light boundary such as “Educational only; persistent or concerning symptoms deserve professional assessment.” Escalation or crisis content must use South African emergency and support routes.

## Four-week launch rhythm

Publish three useful pieces per week, adapted across Instagram and Facebook. Link every post with a distinct UTM.

### Week 1 — reintroduction

- Carousel: “Nine symptoms worth tracking before your next appointment.”
- Reel: 30-second screen recording of a Vida daily check-in.
- Founder note: “Why Vida went quiet, what changed, and why we are rebuilding honestly.”

### Week 2 — first value

- Carousel: “Perimenopause, menopause and post-menopause in plain language.”
- Demonstration: turn seven days of check-ins into a structured conversation starter.
- Community question: “Which symptom was hardest to connect to menopause?” Do not imply a diagnosis.

### Week 3 — trust

- Source-led post: what NICE says about menopause-specific CBT.
- Privacy post: what Vida stores, what it never sells, and how deletion works.
- Build-in-public note showing one change made from founding-member feedback.

### Week 4 — habit

- Seven-day check-in challenge with no prize or medical promise.
- Carousel: “Three questions to take to a healthcare appointment.”
- Honest month-one recap: visits, signups, check-ins and what was learned, using real aggregates only.

## UTM convention

Use lowercase values:

`utm_source=instagram|facebook|newsletter|partner`

`utm_medium=organic_social|email|referral`

`utm_campaign=relaunch`

`utm_content=<short-post-name>`

Do not post an untagged campaign link. Keep one row per asset in the publishing ledger with date, channel, topic, URL, source link, status and observed results.

## Weekly operating loop

Monday: read the prior week’s funnel, choose one bottleneck, and finish one source-led article or refresh.

Tuesday: create the anchor carousel and derivative caption.

Wednesday: publish the carousel; respond manually and warmly to genuine comments without giving medical advice.

Thursday: publish the product demonstration or founder update.

Friday: publish the community prompt, record results, and decide what to repeat, revise or stop.

## Release gates

- A person checks every health claim and link before publishing.
- A real testimonial requires explicit written permission and approved wording.
- No paid media until the free funnel shows repeat check-ins from organic users.
- No automated outreach or account creation with reused credentials.
- Account handles, bios and the first three posts should be prepared together so no empty public account is launched.
